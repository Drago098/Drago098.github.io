In production · serving real users

Dog nose-print biometric ID

A dog's nose print is as unique as a fingerprint. The system identifies an enrolled dog from a phone photo or video.

  • Two-stage pipeline: a detector crops the nose, a deep metric-learning model turns it into a compact embedding, matched against each dog's gallery. A face model breaks close calls.
  • Trained with contrastive and margin-based losses; stays above 96% Rank-1 under every image corruption tested.
  • Benchmarked 6+ variants including a distilled lightweight model; chose the final one on robustness, not clean accuracy.
  • Prototype to production: REST API, async inference workers, mobile app. Now building weekly retraining with a model registry.

When does retrieval help small language models answer medical questions, and what happens when the examples are poisoned?

Accepted · ICISS 2027 · presenting January 2027

How Retrieved Demonstrations Affect Small Language Models on Medical MCQs?

Shubham Das, Ayush Pandey, Ujjawal Jaiswal · IIEST Shibpur · Springer LNCS

Do small language models get better at medical exam questions when you show them retrieved, solved examples? We ran four models (1B to 4B parameters) on all 1,273 MedQA (USMLE) test questions: 84 runs and 106,932 predictions, varying how similar the examples are and whether their answer keys are correct.
